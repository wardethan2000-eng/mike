# 7. Workflows for our practice

**Size: small each.** No new machinery — this is content, not engineering.

## What a workflow is

A saved set of instructions that changes how Mike behaves for a particular job.
The Grounded Reviewer is one: it replaces the normal posture with a strict one
that answers only from documents actually read, and says "the documents provided
do not address this" rather than reaching.

The catalogue, the import, the selection on a conversation — all built. What is
missing is a shelf of them for the work this firm actually does.

## Candidates

- **Reviewing an agreement against our standard form.** Pairs with document
  comparison: what differs, what it means, what to push back on.
- **First read of a new matter.** Given the file, produce the parties, the key
  dates, what is being claimed and what is missing.
- **Demand letters** from our own precedent, with the facts pulled from the
  matter documents.
- **Preparing to depose.** From the pleadings and documents: the themes, the
  witnesses, the documents to put to each.
- **Discovery responses**, answered strictly from the documents on file.

## How to build one well

Write it, then run the same real question with and without it and compare. That
is how the Grounded Reviewer was checked, and it is what turns "sounds better"
into "is better". Keep them in the repository so they are versioned and can be
improved from experience.

## What to watch

- **Strictness has a price.** Tested side by side, the Grounded Reviewer gave up
  a correct answer to avoid the risk of a confident wrong one. Right for advice
  that leaves the building, wrong for orientation. Say which is which in each
  workflow.
- **A workflow cannot make it read what it was not given.** These change how
  Mike reasons, not what it can reach.

## Done when

Three workflows for real recurring jobs are in the catalogue, each tested
against the ordinary behaviour on a real question, with the difference written
down.
